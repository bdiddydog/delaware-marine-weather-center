How to Read Today's Marine Charts

LEGEND / EDUCATION

Surface Analysis

  • H / L: high / low sea-level pressure; nearby numbers usually in hPa (mb).
  • Orange curved lines: isobars, equal atmospheric pressure. On OPC charts, abbreviated labels such as 36 can signify 1036 mb according to the surrounding pattern.
  • Blue line + triangles: cold front; triangles point toward advance.
  • Red line + semicircles: warm front.
  • Purple front: occluded front; alternating red/blue symbols on opposite sides indicate stationary front.
  • GALE: warning for gale-force winds in the indicated offshore region—not automatically a warning for Delaware Bay.

Wind & Wave Chart

  • Numbers over water: significant wave height; check chart header for meters or feet.
  • Significant height: average of the highest third of waves, not the largest possible wave.
  • 1 m ≈ 3.28 ft: 2 m ≈ 6.6 ft; 5 m ≈ 16.4 ft; 7 m ≈ 23 ft.
  • Wave period: seconds between successive crests; not shown on every height chart.
  • Wave direction: check a separate swell-direction product; height by itself does not establish coastal impact.

500-mb Upper-Air Chart

  • Contours: geopotential heights, often labeled in decameters (e.g. 564 = 5,640 m).
  • Trough: equatorward dip in the height field; often associated with unsettled weather.
  • Ridge: poleward bulge; often associated with relatively stable patterns.
  • Wind barbs: short feather = 5 kt; full feather = 10 kt; pennant = 50 kt.
  • Important: 500-mb H/L features are not surface pressure centers. Compare with the surface chart.
